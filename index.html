A cubic zero-free boundary

Let B be the unique root in [5/6, 7/8] of

7884B3 − 18819B2 + 14643B − 3686 = 0.

Then 0.874957019420 < B < 0.874957019421, and

ζ(s) ≠ 0   whenever   Re(s) > B and s ≠ 1.

For every positive integer q and Dirichlet character χ modulo q, L(s,χ) ≠ 0 for Re(s) > B, excluding χ principal and s = 1.

Why the argument works

An arithmetic probe has two descriptions. Direct estimates bound its growth; a transformed expression contains a reciprocal L-function. A zero beyond the claimed boundary would create a pole that the growth estimates make impossible.

The extension lowers a geometric restriction on the moment parameter κ. If β is the relevant rightmost-zero supremum and β > B, choose κ = 2β−1. Write Δ = β−B > 0. The exact endpoint estimates have zero margin at one critical amplitude class. Moving κ to its actual value costs at most Δ/4 in the count exponent, or hΔ/4 physically, where h < 1. The other losses and a strict saving fit within Δ/2. The positive contradiction gap therefore survives even at the critical class.

The balance of the three scalar estimates yields the displayed cubic. The obstruction is sharp for these specified estimates; it does not exclude an improvement using stronger arithmetic information. The earlier verified 0.87499 theorem supplies the bootstrap, without assuming this endpoint.
